The Rabbit Footprint

Tracing rabbit record, abundance and environmental patterns across Australia

The visualisation explores decades of rabbit monitoring data. Map charts reveal geographical patterns in survey coverage. Other visualisations compare records, abundance and environmental condition on rabbit recording. Koonamore survey provide closer look at warren activity.

How to read the data: Occurrence records show where rabbits were reported. Abundance surveys measure rabbits observed per kilometre. Note: More records don't mean more rabbits

What Do Rabbit Records Tell Us?

A rise in recorded rabbit sightings does not necessarily mean rabbit populations increased. Understanding when records were collected, who contributed them, and how abundance was surveyed helps us interpret the evidence.

How Have Rabbit Occurrence Records Changed Over Time?

Explore how the number of recorded occurrences changed across the years

Occurrence records surged in later monitoring years. The database combine records from multiple monitoring programmes. Increased reporting could explain the spike rather than population growth (Roy‐Dufresne et al., 2019).

Who Contributed Australia's Rabbit Records?

Compare the contributions of different data sources

Records come from various sources and monitoring methods. Differences in method and effort explain why some sources dominate the database.

How Have Rabbit Abundance Rankings Changed?

Compare state and territory rankings using mean surveyed rabbits per kilometre

ACT consistently ranked first across all three periods. South Australia rose from fourth to second. Western Australia dropped from second to fourth.

Where and When Were Rabbits Recorded?

Occurrence records are unevenly distributed across Australia. First, compare state totals and occupied survey grid cells; then examine how reporting changed over time.

Which States Have the Most Rabbit Occurrence Records?

One rabbit icon represents 10,000 occurrence records. Icon counts are rounded down; exact totals appear beside each state

State totals represent recorded occurrences, not rabbit population size. Differences may reflect uneven monitoring coverage and efforts.

Where Have Rabbits Been Recorded Across Australia?

Each dot represents one occupied survey grid cell; multiple occurrence records can share a cell.

Survey locations span various Australian environments. The distribution reflect recorded monitoring location and not rabbit coverage.

How Have Rabbit Records Changed Across States and Years?

Darker cells represent more occurrence records for a given state and year, not necessarily more rabbits living there

Recording intensity varies between states and yeears. Monitoring differences may influence these patterns.

How Many Rabbits Are There?

Occurrence records show where rabbits were reported. Abundance surveys offer a different perspective by measuring rabbits observed per kilometre. Survey coverage and the spread of observations are important when comparing results.

Where Was Rabbit Survey Coverage Greatest?

Larger circles indicate grid cells with more reported survey transects, not necessarily more rabbits

Monitoring was conducted across various locations and conditions. Survey coverage is not confirmed to be uniform.

Where Was Surveyed Rabbit Abundance Highest?

Map colours represent mean surveyed rabbits per kilometre, not total rabbit populations

Queensland recorded the highest mean surveyed rabbit abundance. Followed by the northern territories while southern states showed lower averages.

How Does Rabbit Abundance Vary Across Seasons?

Examine the distribution of recorded rabbits per kilometre across survey seasons.

Most surveys recorded relatively low rabbit abundance across seasons. Season 2 showed the highest outlier and greatest extreme.

How Does Rainfall Relate to Rabbit Abundance?

Rabbit abundance may vary with environmental conditions, but rainfall alone cannot explain every survey result. This chart examines the concentration of observations across long-term average rainfall and surveyed abundance.

Where Do Rainfall and Abundance Observations Concentrate?

Each hexagon groups survey observations within a rainfall–abundance range. Darker colours mean more observations, not higher rabbit abundance

Climate information was linked with rabbit monitoring records. Most observations cluster at moderate rainfall and low rabbit abundance.

A Closer Look at Koonamore

Rabbit records reveal their presence across Australia. At Koonamore, researchers also monitored underground rabbit warrens, providing a closer look at local activity.

How Have Rabbit Warrens Changed at Koonamore?

Bubble size represents the average recorded warren count for each decade, not the number of individual rabbits.

Average recorded warren counts were highest during 1970s. Counts decline substantially across subsequent decades.

A Closer Look at December Surveys

Each point represents the average number of recorded warrens in December surveys for that decade.

Warren averages dropped sharply after the 1970s. The lowest average occurred in the 2000s.

Does More Monitoring Reveal More Rabbit Warrens?

Each bubble represents a decade. Bubble size indicates the number of surveys.

Greater survey effort did not consistently correspond with more warrens. The 1970s recorded the highest average despite fewer survey hours.

What Does the Rabbit Footprint Reveal?

Rabbit record reveal widespread monitoring across Australia. Recording varies between regions and years. More records do not indicate larger rabbit population.

Abundance survey reveal variation across states and seasons. Koonamore charts provide insight into local warren activity. The visualisation shows the variation in rabbit monitoring in Australia.
