AN AUSTRALIAN ENVIRONMENTAL DATA VISUALISATION

The Rabbit Footprint

Exploring rabbit distribution, recording patterns and environmental influences across Australia.

European rabbits are widespread across the Australian landscape. However, recorded sightings do not equal population estimates. Survey coverage also differs between regions and years.

This visualisation explores rabbit occurrence, abundance and environmental patterns. Together, these datasets reveal patterns and important limitations.

How to read the data: Occurrence records show where rabbits were reported. Abundance surveys measure rabbits observed per kilometre. Neither dataset provides a complete rabbit population census.

TWhat Do Rabbit Records Tell Us?

Before examining where rabbits occur, we first need to understand how the evidence was collected. A dramatic rise in records does not necessarily mean a dramatic rise in rabbit numbers.

How Many Rabbits Are There?

Occurrence data shows where rabbits have been reported. Abundance surveys provide a different perspective by measuring rabbits observed per kilometre. These measurements help us compare survey outcomes across places and seasons.

Extreme survey values influence the overall seasonal distributions. Median values provide a more representative comparison.

Extreme observations shape the distribution. Some surveys recorded much higher rabbits-per- kilometre values than most others. Compare the medians and the spread of observations rather than relying only on the highest points.

How Does Rainfall Relate to Rabbit Abundance

Rabbit abundance may vary with environmental conditions, but individual factors rarely tell the whole story. Our final visualisation examines how surveyed abundance relates to long-term average rainfall.

Similar rainfall levels can produce different abundance observations. The regression line shows association, not causation.

What Does the Rabbit Footprint Reveal?

Rabbit records provide valuable evidence of occurrence across Australia, but they also reveal the uneven nature of monitoring. Differences in data sources, survey locations and observation methods shape the patterns visible in the database.

By separating occurrence records from abundance measurements, we gain a more careful view of rabbit distribution and environmental variation. The key lesson is that understanding an invasive species requires understanding the data used to describe it.
